Research

Experimental and simulation work in neutral-atom quantum systems.

Master’s project - IIT Madras, 2025–2026

Scalable simulation of multi-mode open quantum dynamics

Supervisor: Prof. Athreya Shankar

Exact simulation of open quantum systems is prohibitively expensive for more than one or two modes: the density matrix grows exponentially with the number of modes. We developed a scalable alternative using a sum-of-Gaussians (SoG) variational ansatz - a superposition of displaced, squeezed Gaussian states - evolved under the McLachlan time-dependent variational principle (TDVP). The computational cost scales linearly with the number of modes.

Dissipative dynamics are handled using the quantum trajectories (MCWF) method. Time evolution reduces to computing analytic single-mode Gaussian expectation values, derived and implemented as a core part of this work. The approach is well-suited to experimentally relevant regimes that are hard to treat exactly: sideband cooling of ions confined in a 2D harmonic trap at large Lamb-Dicke parameters, and neutral-atom dynamics in optical dipole traps. As a simple benchmark we use 2D sideband cooling, compared against QuTiP.

Master’s project - IIT Madras, 2025–2026

Evolution of neutral atoms in optical tweezers under resonant imaging

Supervisors: Prof. Athreya Shankar & Prof. Monika Aidelsburger

Studied the dynamics of a neutral atom trapped in a Gaussian optical tweezer imaged using a fast-imaging scheme (alternating counter-propagating near-resonant pulses). Studied effect of recoil heating and proposed a more accurate loss model to explain experimental results from Falconi et al. PRL 2025.

Master’s project - Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics, 2025–2026

Zeeman slower and 2D MOT design for a Yb-171 apparatus

Supervisor: Prof. Monika Aidelsburger

Designed a Zeeman slower (ZS) followed by a 2D magneto-optical trap for loading cold Yb-171 atoms into a tweezer platform, both on the broad 399 nm transition. The goal was to maximise the capture velocity into the 2D MOT given realistic beam powers and permanent-magnet geometry constraints.

Semiclassical trajectory simulations compute the radiation-pressure force along each atomic path. Beam detunings, powers, and varying magnetic field stacks and positions were optimised via Bayesian optimisation (scikit-optimize), iteratively for the 2DMOT and ZS, maximising the fraction of atoms captured into the MOT.

Laser locking and optical tweezers of Raman beams for a Rb quantum processor

Supervisor: Prof. Alexandre Cooper-Roy

Worked on two problems: generating optical tweezers using an acousto-optic deflector (AOD) for a Raman laser, and phase-locking two Raman lasers using an optical phase-locked loop (OPLL).
