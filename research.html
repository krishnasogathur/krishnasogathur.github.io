Master’s project - IIT Madras, 2024–2025

Evolution of neutral atoms in optical tweezers under resonant imaging

Studied the dynamics of a neutral atom trapped in a Gaussian optical tweezer imaged using a fast-imaging scheme (alternating counter-propagating near-resonant pulses). Each scattered photon imparts ±ℏk recoil and drives internal state transitions, causing a competition between trapping and heating that eventually leads to atom loss. This process limits the fidelity of non-destructive measurements in tweezer-based quantum computing platforms.

Master’s project - IIT Madras, 2024–2025

Zeeman slower and 2D MOT design for a Yb-171 apparatus

Designed the cold-atom loading chain for a Yb-171 tweezer platform: an atomic Zeeman slower (ZS) followed by a 2D magneto-optical trap, both on the broad 399 nm transition. The goal was to maximise the capture velocity into the 2D MOT given realistic beam powers and permanent-magnet geometry constraints.

Semiclassical trajectory simulations compute the radiation-pressure force along each atomic path. Two scattering-rate models (shared saturation and effective seff) bracket the true multi-beam rate. Beam detunings, powers, and magnet configurations (N=5 or N=6 segments) were optimised jointly via two-stage Bayesian optimisation (scikit-optimize), maximising the fraction of atoms captured into the MOT.

Master’s project - IIT Madras, 2024–2025

Novel techniques for open quantum system dynamics: multi-mode laser cooling

Exact simulation of open quantum systems is prohibitively expensive for more than one or two modes: the density matrix grows exponentially with the number of modes. We developed a scalable alternative using a sum-of-Gaussians (SoG) variational ansatz - a superposition of displaced, squeezed Gaussian states - evolved under the McLachlan time-dependent variational principle (TDVP). The state is non-Gaussian overall, and accuracy improves with the number of Gaussian components, but the computational cost scales as O(NGauss × d) rather than exponentially with mode number d.

Open-system dissipation is handled via quantum trajectories (MCWF): between jumps, the state evolves under a non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian via TDVP; at each jump event, the jump operator is applied analytically within the Gaussian manifold. All matrix elements - overlaps, force vectors, Gram matrices - reduce to analytic single-mode Gaussian expectation values, derived and implemented as a core part of this work. Benchmarked against QuTiP on Kerr oscillators and 2D sideband cooling trajectories.
